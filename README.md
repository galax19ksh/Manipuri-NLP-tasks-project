# Cross-Lingual-Manipuri-NLP
BTech major project
This thesis addresses the significant challenges in developing Natural Language Processing (NLP) applications for low-resource languages, with a specific focus on Manipuri (Meiteilon). As a tonal and agglutinative language with limited digital resources, Manipuri presents unique linguistic and computational difficulties that hinder the application of conventional NLP techniques. To mitigate these issues, this research explores cross-lingual and transfer learning approaches, leveraging knowledge and resources from high-resource languages such as English. The central methodology involves employing FastText word embeddings in conjunction with sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) model architectures, including LSTM, biLSTM, GRU, and Transformer, to perform key NLP tasks such as machine translation, part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, and sentiment analysis.
The experimental framework includes multilingual pre-training, fine-tuning on limited Manipuri datasets, and data augmentation techniques such as back-translation. Comparative analyses demonstrate the varying effectiveness of each seq2seq model architecture, with Transformers showing superior performance in capturing long-range dependencies, while LSTM-based models offer robust results in low-data regimes. The study also evaluates the impact of synthetic data generation and multilingual embeddings on model accuracy across tasks.
The findings contribute to the growing body of research in low-resource NLP by proposing practical methodologies that combine linguistic insight with deep learning. This work not only advances the computational representation of Manipuri but also provides a scalable framework applicable to other underrepresented languages, ultimately supporting efforts in language preservation and digital inclusion.
